# Shimano Dura-Ace coverage

The catalogue covers the original Dura-Ace road range and each subsequent road generation through R9200, with separate records for mechanical and Di2 offerings. It also includes Dura-Ace 10 and the 7600/7710 track range. There are 15 groupset records and 142 component identities across 165 memberships. This is generation coverage, not a claim that every part, finish, mounting option or gear combination has been catalogued.

## Dates and families

[Shimano’s Dura-Ace history](https://www.shimano.com/en/100th/history/products/14.html) is the primary source for release dates through R9100/R9150.

The 1973 brochure does not present a numbered series. Its catalogue model label is “Original”; retrospective “7100” naming is not applied to individual parts. [The archived original brochure](https://www.sheldonbrown.com/shimano1973/) documents the family, but it does not satisfy the requirement for a manufacturer-hosted component technical document. No model numbers or technical-document links have been invented to fill this gap.

For 7800, the release year follows Shimano’s product history (2004). Shimano’s corporate timeline also records its development in 2003; these are different events. R9150 follows the same history’s 2016 date, rather than assigning a separate year from later retail availability. The [R9200 announcement](https://www.shimano.com/en/csr/news/news_detail_1.html) documents release in autumn 2021. The 7600 track family’s 1985 start is supported by the November 1985 date on [Shimano’s FC-7600 exploded view](https://si.shimano.com/en/pdfs/ev/FC-7600-0719/EV-FC-7600-0719A.pdf); 7710 parts are later members, not claimed to have launched in 1985. Only the documented start year is recorded; end years are not collected.

- [2008 Shimano Road consumer catalogue, PDF page 7](https://sportresort.ru/pdf/eng2008_Consumer_Road.pdf#page=7).

- [2011 Shimano product line-up](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2011_LINE-UP_CHART.pdf).
- [2014–2015 specifications](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2014-2015_Specifications_v020_en.pdf).
- [2017–2018 specifications](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2017-2018_Specifications_v028_en.pdf).
- [2025–2026 specifications](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2025-2026_Specifications_v035_en.pdf) and [product line-up](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2025-2026_Line-up_chart_v033_en.pdf).

Shared cranksets, cassettes, pedals and bottom brackets keep one identity across mechanical/Di2 offerings. The 2005 specification table (PDF page 118) lists BB-7700 and BB-7710 for the FC-7710 track crank. A shared membership does not imply that all parts in those groupsets are interchangeable. Time-trial controls are component variants within their parent Di2 series. Brake-system variants and anniversary finishes do not create extra generations.

Dura-Ace 10 means **10 mm chain pitch**, not ten sprockets. Its crank belongs to that dedicated track system; do not assume compatibility with conventional 12.7 mm chain-pitch track equipment. Likewise, a 7400-series membership is not a claim of universal SIS cable-pull compatibility across later generations. Consult the linked service instructions for a proposed combination.

## Archived catalogue scans

[La Bible du Vélo’s Dura-Ace history](https://labibleduvelocataloguesshimano.blogspot.com/p/dura-ace-history.html) is a useful index to period material. Catalogue facts below come from the actual manufacturer scans, rather than the history article’s narrative or assembled groupset photographs. Shimano’s own chronology remains the basis for family start years.

- [Catalogue archived under 1982](https://labibleduvelocataloguesshimano.blogspot.com/p/shimano-1982.html): printed page 13 identifies RD-7300 at 214 g, with a 26T largest sprocket and 13T maximum front difference. It does not establish total capacity or the smallest sprocket. Printed page 16 gives SP-7300 at 224 g for the 26.8 mm A-type and BL-7300 at 107 g **each**, including the pad. Printed page 20 establishes RD-7200’s 13T front difference.
- [Catalogue archived under 1989](https://labibleduvelocataloguesshimano.blogspot.com/p/shimano-1989.html): the road drivetrain spread (image 7) lists FC-7402, FD-7400, RD-7402, CS-7400-8, the two pedal models, later downtube controls and FH-7402. The following road/track spread (image 8) identifies HP-7400, the distinct SP-7400-A/B seatposts and the FC-7600/BB-7600 configurations. Each updated record includes both the archive page and exact scan image so these references remain identifiable.

The new 7400-series identities are FC-7402, PD-7400, PD-7401, HP-7400, SP-7400-A, SP-7400-B, FH-7402, SL-7401-FCAI and SL-7402-FCAI. They supplement the existing family without creating another groupset or changing existing IDs. Shimano-hosted exploded drawings cover all nine additions.

## Weights and remaining gaps

72 component records have sourced weights compliant with the [weight guidelines](weights-guidelines.md), including six added by the [catalogue/handbook review](dealer-catalogues.md). The other 70 retain explicit null weights. Null means an acceptable reference configuration has not yet been recorded, not that the component cannot be weighed.

Crank weights identify arm length and chainrings and exclude the bottom bracket. FC-7800 remains unknown because the 2008 figure includes the bottom bracket; it is not reduced by an estimated bearing weight. Front-derailleur weights identify the mount, including the 28.6 mm clamp for the FD-7400-B reference. Shifter weights are published pair weights. The new SL-7401-FCAI and SL-7402-FCAI entries remain null because their source figures do not explicitly establish a pair. BL-7300’s single-lever figure is not doubled. FD-7300 also remains null: the catalogue lists two band sizes without identifying the weighed clamp. Cassette weights identify speed count and tooth range; lockring inclusion is assumed under the contribution policy. Quick-release hub weights require a source separating or excluding the skewer. The 1991 catalogue explicitly labels FH-7402-8’s 460 g as including quick release, so it remains null. Large-flange HB-7600 front/rear weights now use the 1991 nut-fastened, hollow-axle references: 241 g front and 313 g rear including lockring. These track hubs use axle nuts rather than quick-release skewers; no skewer mass was subtracted. Other track-hub weights and the 2001 hub figures remain withheld without qualifying evidence.

Published average weights can differ between specification editions. Each numeric weight cites the edition and reference configuration used; values from different editions have not been averaged. Unknown rear-derailleur capacity limits remain null and display as unsourced. RD-7800-SS follows the 14T front difference in SI-5V50E; its record notes that the 2008 consumer catalogue instead lists 16T.

Still to add: manufacturer-documented original 1973 components, further early track parts, additional cage/mount/chainring/cassette variants, and missing configurations or weights. Brake calipers now have [initial records and documented gaps](brake-calipers.md); chains, fixed track sprockets, batteries and complete wheelsets are outside the current component taxonomy. Their omission does not imply they were absent from a groupset.

Standalone brake-lever models and their source limitations are documented in the [brake-lever source notes](brake-levers.md). Integrated shift/brake controls remain in the Shifters category.

The [1991 dealer catalogue review](shimano-1991.md) resolves FC-7402’s selected SG configuration, adds the 90 g HP-7600 track headset and documents the large-flange HB-7600 references. The ST-7400 560 g figure is still not used as a pair weight without explicit pair evidence.

The [2005 handbook review](shimano-2005.md) recovers eight Dura-Ace-member weights, including BR-7800 **314 g per pair**, ST-7800 **419 g per pair**, FH-7800 **264 g without QR**, and FC-7703 **681 g** for 170 mm, 53/39/30T without its separate bottom bracket.
