# Shimano Dura-Ace coverage

The catalogue covers the original Dura-Ace road range and each subsequent road generation through R9200, with separate records for mechanical and Di2 offerings. It also includes Dura-Ace 10 and the 7600/7710 track range. There are 15 groupset records and 123 component identities across 146 memberships. This is generation coverage, not a claim that every part, finish, mounting option or gear combination has been catalogued.

## Dates and families

[Shimano’s Dura-Ace history](https://www.shimano.com/en/100th/history/products/14.html) is the primary source for release dates through R9100/R9150.

| Dura-Ace AX 7300 | 1980 | Aerodynamic components |
| Dura-Ace 7400 | 1984 | Original SIS, 7401/7402 updates and later STI components |
| Dura-Ace 7700 | 1996 | Nine-speed double and later triple components |
| Dura-Ace 7800 | 2004 | Ten-speed double/triple, revised controls and carbon crank variant |

The 1973 brochure does not present a numbered series. Its catalogue model label is “Original”; retrospective “7100” naming is not applied to individual parts. [The archived original brochure](https://www.sheldonbrown.com/shimano1973/) documents the family, but it does not satisfy the requirement for a manufacturer-hosted component technical document. No model numbers or technical-document links have been invented to fill this gap.

For 7800, the release year follows Shimano’s product history (2004). Shimano’s corporate timeline also records its development in 2003; these are different events. R9150 follows the same history’s 2016 date, rather than assigning a separate year from later retail availability. The [R9200 announcement](https://www.shimano.com/en/csr/news/news_detail_1.html) documents release in autumn 2021. The 7600 track family’s 1985 start is supported by the November 1985 date on [Shimano’s FC-7600 exploded view](https://si.shimano.com/en/pdfs/ev/FC-7600-0719/EV-FC-7600-0719A.pdf); 7710 parts are later members, not claimed to have launched in 1985. Only the documented start year is recorded; end years are not collected.

## Component sources and identity

Every listed component links to a Shimano-hosted exploded view, service instruction or specification PDF. Period catalogues and later specification tables supply the additional facts attributed to them in each record. The principal reference editions are:

- [December 1987 Shimano catalogue, Dura-Ace component specifications](https://www.disraeligears.co.uk/site/shimano_bicycle_system_components_december_1987_-_scan_10.html).
- [2001 Shimano catalogue, pages 28–29](https://www.charlie-taylor.net/bikes/library/shimano/2001/28-29.htm).
- [2008 Shimano Road consumer catalogue, PDF page 7](https://sportresort.ru/pdf/eng2008_Consumer_Road.pdf#page=7).
- [2005 Shimano specifications, PDF page 118](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2005_SPECIFICATION.pdf#page=118).
- [2011 Shimano product line-up](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2011_LINE-UP_CHART.pdf).
- [2014–2015 specifications](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2014-2015_Specifications_v020_en.pdf).
- [2017–2018 specifications](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2017-2018_Specifications_v028_en.pdf).
- [2025–2026 specifications](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2025-2026_Specifications_v035_en.pdf) and [product line-up](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2025-2026_Line-up_chart_v033_en.pdf).

Shared cranksets, cassettes, pedals and bottom brackets keep one identity across mechanical/Di2 offerings. The 2005 specification table (PDF page 118) lists BB-7700 and BB-7710 for the FC-7710 track crank. A shared membership does not imply that all parts in those groupsets are interchangeable. Time-trial controls are component variants within their parent Di2 series. Brake-system variants and anniversary finishes do not create extra generations.

Dura-Ace 10 means **10 mm chain pitch**, not ten sprockets. Its crank belongs to that dedicated track system; do not assume compatibility with conventional 12.7 mm chain-pitch track equipment. Likewise, a 7400-series membership is not a claim of universal SIS cable-pull compatibility across later generations. Consult the linked service instructions for a proposed combination.

## Weights and remaining gaps

37 component records have sourced weights compliant with the [weight guidelines](weights-guidelines.md). The other 86 retain explicit null weights. Null means an acceptable reference configuration has not yet been recorded, not that the component cannot be weighed.

Crank weights identify arm length and chainrings and exclude the bottom bracket. FC-7800 remains unknown because the 2008 figure includes the bottom bracket; it is not reduced by an estimated bearing weight. Front-derailleur weights identify braze-on mounting. Shifter weights are published pair weights. Cassette weights identify speed count and tooth range; lockring inclusion is assumed under the contribution policy. Hub weights are used only where Shimano separately lists the hub and quick-release skewer. The 2001 hub figures and track hub figures have not been treated as skewer-free measurements without evidence.

Published average weights can differ between specification editions. Each numeric weight cites the edition and reference configuration used; values from different editions have not been averaged. Unknown rear-derailleur capacity limits remain null and display as unsourced. RD-7800-SS follows the 14T front difference in SI-5V50E; its record notes that the 2008 consumer catalogue instead lists 16T.

Still to add: manufacturer-documented original 1973 components, further early track parts, additional cage/mount/chainring/cassette variants, and missing configurations or weights. Brake calipers, chains, fixed track sprockets, batteries and complete wheelsets are outside the current component taxonomy. Their omission does not imply they were absent from a groupset.
