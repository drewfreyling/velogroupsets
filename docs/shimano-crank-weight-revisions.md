# Shimano crankset weight revisions and power meters

Reviewed on **28 November 2026**. Four additional model-specific cranksets have qualified weights in Shimano’s **2025–2026 specifications v3.5**, dated 15 April 2026. Existing component identities, positive weights and primary documents remain unchanged.

| Component | Weighed configuration | Weight | BCD / mount | Weight reference |

| [FC-M9125-1](../data/components/shimano-fc-m9125-1.json) | 170 mm, 32T, excludes bottom bracket | 578 g | Direct mount | [PDF 49 / printed 46](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2025-2026_Specifications_v035_en.pdf#page=49) |

All three weight-row headings explicitly specify **170 mm, w/o bottom bracket**. The selected chainring combinations appear alongside their masses. These are actual manufacturer reference configurations under the [crankset guidelines](weights-guidelines.md#cranksets); no crank-length conversion, bottom-bracket subtraction or battery deduction is used. Other arm lengths and chainring options do not inherit these weights.

## FC-M8000-B1: later qualified reference

The [earlier review](shimano-mtb-derailleur-weights.md#reference-left-unresolved) withheld the internally inconsistent 2017–2018 entry: its available rings were 30T/32T but its 698 g reference named 36T. That earlier figure remains unused. The later 2025–2026 column explicitly pairs **669 g with 30T**, resolving the missing qualified reference through a separate source rather than an assumed correction.

The [2018 Deore XT M8000 range chart, PDF/printed page 27](https://na.s-tec.shimano.com/s3_assets/filelibrary/Catalogs/2018_TSS_MANUAL_EN.pdf#page=27) lists FC-M8000-B1 with 30T/32T. The [manufacturer drawing](https://si.shimano.com/en/pdfs/ev/FC-M8000-3849/EV-FC-M8000-3849B.pdf) explicitly includes B1/B2. The exact single-ring specification column gives **96 mm BCD** and **53.4 mm chainline**, 3 mm outboard of the standard crank. The double-ring B2 reference retains its separate identity, weight and 96/64 mm bolt circles.

## XTR and power-meter range membership

The [2025–2026 M9100 chart, PDF 7 / printed 5](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2025-2026_Line-up_chart_v033_en.pdf#page=7) explicitly lists FC-M9125-1 with 32T/34T for Enduro and 148 mm rear spacing. Its exact specification column gives a **55 mm chainline** and **Direct** chainring mounting; no numerical BCD is invented. The [FC-M9125-1 drawing](https://si.shimano.com/en/pdfs/ev/FC-M9125-4884/EV-FC-M9125-4884.pdf) confirms its identity and ring assembly. FC-M9130-1 remains a separate existing reference.

The [Ultegra R8100/R8150 chart, PDF 55 / printed 53](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2025-2026_Line-up_chart_v033_en.pdf#page=55) lists **FC-R8100-P** with 50/34T or 52/36T, as well as a without-chainrings option. The selected 758 g reference explicitly includes 50/34T rings; it is not the ringless configuration. Its [exact drawing](https://si.shimano.com/en/pdfs/ev/FC-R8100-P-4905/EV-FC-R8100-P-4905B.pdf) specifies 110 mm BCD.

**FC-R9200-PX** appears in the [R9100 range chart continuation, PDF 54 / printed 52](https://productinfo.shimano.com/pdfs/product/archive/2025-2026_Line-up_chart_v033_en.pdf#page=54), following the R9100 heading on PDF page 53. The new record therefore references the existing R9100 groupset. Its model number does not establish R9200 groupset membership. The chart and weight table differ on a larger-ring option (54/42T versus 54/40T); this review selects their consistent **50/34T** reference and makes no claim about that other option. Rear-speed count is not inferred from the model code or transferred from FC-R9200-P. The [exact PX drawing](https://si.shimano.com/en/pdfs/ev/FC-R9200-PX-4994/EV-FC-R9200-PX-4994B.pdf) confirms the distinct model and 110 mm BCD. The existing FC-R9200-P record remains unchanged even though its selected reference also weighs 745 g.
