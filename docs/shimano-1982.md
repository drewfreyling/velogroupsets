# Shimano April 1982: full catalogue gap review

**22 November follow-up:** the [early-model import](shimano-early-followup-2026-11-22.md) resolves further inventory/document-link gaps under the optional-link policy, adding 26 components and five weights across the reviewed editions. The original review below records the earlier batch and its findings.


Reviewed on 21 November 2026 from the [owner-supplied original Shimano Bicycle System Components catalogue](https://drive.google.com/file/d/17QiI_JPy9NX1NiRCOWtYQDXxmGbQAJ5N/view). The scan contains **144 PDF pages**. Its final page states copyright April 1982 and print code `0482 C1/22M`; PDF metadata is not used as its publication date. For the numbered product sections, printed page numbers are PDF page numbers minus three.

This follows the earlier [600 EX review](shimano-600-ex-1982.md), rather than repeating that import. It adds **43 component records**, **two groupsets** and **12 previously unknown weights**: eleven new-record weights and the existing BB-SL31 reference. It also fills **six rear-derailleur limit fields across four existing Altus records**. The catalogue contains 261 groupsets and 1,561 components after this batch.

Existing component IDs, slugs, model numbers and primary technical-document URLs are preserved. All new primary documents are exact Shimano-hosted assembly drawings. The original Drive scan supports specifications and memberships; it is not an approved alternative primary-document host. No scan bytes or images are committed.

The two new named road offerings are **Adamas AX** and **Shimano AX**. The original system chart on PDF page 138 / printed page 135 explicitly puts Integer AX’s FC-AX21 and Positron AX’s RD-AX10 in the same **Shimano AX Series Road & Touring Ensemble**. They are therefore kept together in that manufacturer-defined group, with their Integer/Positron names retained in component descriptions. Their 1982 start records availability in this reviewed edition, not a claimed exact first launch. The Shimano AX inventory is incomplete; the console/front-derailleur gaps below remain explicit.

## Added inventory without an acceptable weight

- **Dura-Ace AX:** FD-7320 oval E-tube braze-on; SL-7310/7311/7321/7322 control pairs; front/rear FH-7370 assemblies; SP-7320 made-to-order oval C-tube post.
- **600 AX:** SL-6300/6310/6311/6321/6322 control pairs; front/rear FH-6361 assemblies; PD-6300 pedal pair; BL-6300 lever pair; BR-6300 and BR-6310 caliper pairs.
- **Adamas AX:** SL-AD10/AD11 control pairs; front/rear FH-AD65 QR assemblies; FC-AD11, FC-AD21 and FC-AD22 cranksets; PD-AD10/AD20 pedal pairs; BL-AD10 and BL-AD50 lever pairs.
- **Integer AX:** FC-AX21 crankset and the independently manufacturer-documented BB-AX31 assembly.

The Adamas page 81 banner reverses the single/double labels, but its detailed specification text and the Shimano assembly drawing both identify **FC-AD11 as single** and **FC-AD21 as double**. The records follow that corroborated identification rather than the contradictory banner.

Unknown records retain explicit null weights. Shifter and pedal records represent complete pairs, without doubling an individual figure. Standalone levers likewise require pair weights: BL-7300’s 107 g each and BL-6300’s 109 g each are not doubled into canonical pair totals. The Adamas lever figures also do not explicitly establish a complete pair reference.

The BR-6300/6310 table gives one shared **375 g front/rear** figure despite listing different long/short-reach configurations. Both model-specific records remain unweighed until a specific reference resolves which configuration was weighed. The unambiguous BR-7300 and BR-AD20 pair references are retained as complete pairs, not divided into front/rear values.

No new crankset weight meets the arm-length/configuration rule. The Adamas and Integer tables offer partial chainwheel figures and/or multiple arm lengths without selecting the weighed length. No missing crank, protector or BB mass is inferred or subtracted.

## Other source limits and remaining leads

- **PD-DE10:** the 1981 edition gives 570 g, while the 1982 page 75 prints 20.1 oz alongside 550 g. Neither identifies an explicit pair basis. Both citations are retained with a null weight; no conversion, averaging or doubling resolves the inconsistent reference.
- **FD-7300:** 96 g is still not assigned because the table offers different 28.0/28.6 mm clamps without identifying the weighed band. The oval FD-7320 does not inherit FD-7310’s standard round-tube braze-on figure.
- **FH-7261/7271:** the catalogue’s 412 g rear figure says “except cassette gears”, not without QR. It does not replace the exact, explicitly skewer-free SI-M-14 references already in the records. SI-N18 for FH-7370 is an interchangeability document and does not supply a new compliant hub mass.
- **FD-7322, SP-7322 and SP-6322:** listed oval-tube variants, but exact current manufacturer-hosted primary documents were not found. A nearby oval E/C model’s drawing is not substituted.
- **SL-AD12/AD15/AD24, SL-AT12 and the original 600 SL-BC10:** useful control leads still lacking exact manufacturer-hosted primary documents. SL-BC10’s 160 g explicitly paired reference is known from page 69, but is not attached to an unrelated drawing.
- **BB-DE10, BB-SL32 and FD-DE20:** model references in assembly headings/charts still need exact model reconciliation; adjacent assemblies do not establish that they are identical.
- **Original Dura-Ace FC-7110, BB-7200, HP-7100 and HB-7120; original 600 RD-6100/6101, SL-6100 and HB-6120:** explicit catalogue identities, but exact primary-document gaps remain. Shared short/long cage figures are not automatically applied to both.
- **Early/large-flange 600 EX FH-6250/6252/6253/6260/6262/6263:** remain document/configuration leads; null or skewer-inclusive references are not converted into canonical hub weights.
- **General parts and systems:** RS/EG rear derailleurs, FE front derailleurs, general controls/hubs and Tourney TS/TC brakes deserve a separate range/membership review. Their placement in a general product chapter does not make them Adamas, Altus or a newly invented all-purpose groupset.
- **Positron/FF/PPS, coaster/internal-gear systems and BMX:** need review of their system-specific memberships, control assemblies and/or categories before full import. Single console mechanisms are not turned into fictional left/right pairs or declared 1× without evidence. BMX is not silently recategorised to fit the current schema.

This review scans the complete edition and records both accepted additions and unresolved leads; it does not claim every model or technical-document gap has been filled.
