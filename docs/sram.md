# SRAM road and gravel groupsets

The catalogue contains **28 SRAM groupset records and 103 component identities across 118 memberships**. This pass covers road, cyclocross and XPLR gravel. It does not claim every component variant or complete SRAM MTB coverage.

## Generations and dates

Dates identify documented introduction or availability, rather than an inferred marketing model year. The original Force/Rival road launch was in 2006 and RED in 2007, as recorded in [SRAM’s company history](https://www.sram.com/en/company/about/history). Force CX1 began in 2014 and was renamed/expanded as Force 1 in 2015; this is one family record, not two copies of its parts. Apex’s ten-speed offering was available in 2010 even though launch coverage also described 2011 products.

The group `model_number` fields identify the system/generation: 10-speed, 22, 11-speed, D1, D2 or E1. They are not invented complete-kit part numbers. Component `model_number` fields use the actual SRAM Model IDs. A SRAM aftermarket groupset kit can exclude cranks, cassette and bottom bracket; the catalogue describes the documented component range rather than promising the contents of a particular box.

The original publications and contemporary launch reports establish the dates; current manufacturer service pages identify the exact component Model IDs used here. A family’s start year does not date every later reference part to that year. For example, the original RED family starts in 2007, but its RD-RED-A1 reference comes from the manufacturer’s later 2008–2011 parts listing. Force’s B1 controls, RED 22 C2 components, later 36T road AXS derailleurs and expanded cassette options likewise must not all be described as original launch equipment.

## Systems and shared components

- Original RED eTap is **11-speed**. RED, Force and Rival road AXS are **12-speed**; the word eTap alone does not establish interchangeability.
- D1 XPLR and Force D2 XPLR are **12-speed, 1x, 44T-max** systems using a conventional derailleur hanger. They remain separate from the 13-speed Full Mount offering.
- RED XPLR E1 (2024), Force XPLR E1 and Rival XPLR E1 (2025) are **13-speed, 1x, 46T-max Full Mount** systems requiring a UDH frame interface. This does not make every Eagle Transmission component a member of an XPLR group.
- E1 road and XPLR share the documented ED-RED-E1, ED-FRC-E1 and ED-RIV-E1 controls respectively. SRAM’s product-line availability FAQs explicitly name these shared controls. The D1 XPLR launch likewise extended existing road AXS controls. Each complete pair retains one component identity across its documented memberships.
- The 2021 XPLR launch assigns XG-1271 to RED/Force and XG-1251 to Rival. XG-1271 retains its shared identity. PG-1170 is explicitly listed in the Force/Force 1 manufacturer ranges; PG-1130 is listed for Rival 1 and Apex 1. Shared cassette membership does not mean that every cassette range works with every rear derailleur.
- The current Rival road AXS range retains CS-XG-1250-D1. An E1 family does not require inventing an E1 suffix for every retained component.

The manufacturer service pages and technical documents are hosted on `sram.com` or its subdomains. No archive exception is added. Brand ownership alone does not make RockShox, Zipp, Quarq, TRUVATIV or TIME parts SRAM-branded component records.

Cable/housing inclusion is not stated for those mechanical control pair figures. Cassette lockring inclusion follows the project’s convention. Other sprocket options remain model options; the reference weight is not applied to every tooth range.

The other **31 weights remain null**, including the unresolved mechanical Apex XPLR RD-APX-1-D1 reference. Crank references must establish length, chainrings and bottom-bracket exclusion before a figure is published. A manufacturer field labelled “Weight Based On” without an actual weight is not a measurement. Complete hydraulic brake-system figures are not used as control-only pair weights, and individual-side figures are not doubled. All shifter records represent left/right pairs, including records with unknown weights.

For mechanical 1x controls, the left unit is a brake-only lever. The complete curated pair identifies both actual model numbers: SB-FRC-HRD-A1 / BL-FRC-A1, SB-RIV-B1 / BL-RIV-A1 or SB-APX-B1 / BL-APX-A1. These are different assemblies from a two-shifter 2x pair; the known SB-RIV-B1 2x pair weight is not reused for the Rival 1 pair.

## Derailleur limits

All four rear-derailleur limit fields are present. Published fixed largest-sprocket limits are recorded; capacity, smallest sprocket and front tooth difference remain explicitly unknown where not established. When one SRAM Model ID combines multiple cage/maximum-sprocket variants, the scalar largest-sprocket limit stays null and the published options are described separately. A 33T/36T family is not silently turned into a universal 36T rear derailleur.

The [catalogue/handbook review](dealer-catalogues.md) adds the original RD-RED-A1's explicit 2010 reference limits: 33T capacity, 28T largest sprocket, 11T smallest sprocket and 16T front difference. These are not applied to later RED generations or to the combined Force/Rival cage options. The manual supplies no reference weight for this RED assembly.

**SRAM’s “RD Minimum (Cassette)” is the minimum compatible largest sprocket.** For example, the 28T field on a 36T-max road AXS derailleur does not mean its smallest sprocket must have 28 teeth. This information is stored separately as `minimum_largest_sprocket_variants`, never copied into `minimum_sprocket_teeth`. Likewise, a 46T value for Full Mount XPLR is not published as a 46T smallest sprocket. No independent total capacity or front-difference limit is calculated from cage appearance, chainring options or cassette arithmetic.

## Remaining coverage

The original RED family currently has only its documented rear derailleur. Early Force and RED B1 cranksets, some 12-speed XPLR crank variants, power-meter options and additional cage revisions remain gaps. Generic GXP/DUB bottom brackets, wheel hubs, headsets, seatposts and pedals are not assigned to every family just because they could be used with it. Brake calipers are supported but SRAM caliper records remain to be added. Chains, batteries and single flat-bar controls remain outside the current component/pair conventions. Further manufacturer evidence can add these records without changing existing IDs.

## Wide road options

The [additional road review](road-groupsets.md) adds four manufacturer-documented Wide front-derailleur/crankset options to existing Force/Rival AXS D1 families: FD-FRC-EW-D1, FC-FRC-W-D1, FD-RIV-EW-D1 and FC-RIV-W-D1. Their 47.5 mm chainline and 43/30T configuration remain distinct from standard options. All four weights are unknown. Wide equipment is a component option, so this review does not invent another SRAM groupset family.
