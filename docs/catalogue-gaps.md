# Catalogue inventory and specification gaps

Reviewed on **15 November 2026**, after the [Campagnolo starter-inventory review](campagnolo-component-coverage.md). The review adds **68 component identities**, six manufacturer-listed weights and **201 numeric limit fields across 53 rear derailleurs**. All **238 groupsets** now contain at least two sourced components. The catalogue has **1,247 component identities across four manufacturers**.

This establishes broader starter inventories. Complete build kits, further brake generations, frame-specific bottom brackets and many weights still need review. Unknown values remain explicit rather than being estimated from compatibility, cage length or adjacent catalogue columns.

The early Shimano drawings explicitly identify original 600, conventional Dura-Ace, Altus/Selecta and Altus AX assemblies. FC-AT20 from the later MTB Altus A20 range is not transferred into the early road range. New HB-6110 front and HB-7110 rear records retain the printed manufacturer model numbers; position is recorded separately in the identity/description. Their weights remain unknown and must exclude skewers.

The original 1982 Campagnolo US catalogue supplies complete group lists for Nuovo Record, Super Record and Gran Sport. Combined hub-set and lever-plus-caliper brakeset codes are not converted into single-component identities. Race Triple and Champ Triple explicitly include Veloce square-taper bottom brackets in the original 2008 product tables; the matching parts book supplies separate 111/115.5 mm and English/Italian identifiers. Optional Ultra cassettes are shared with the two modern Super Record build configurations that list them.

## Brakes and bottom brackets

The 2017 Campagnolo complete-product tables establish caliper pairs and separate direct-mount front, rear-under-BB and rear-seatstay models. Their exact codes and group assignments are retained. BR02-XE comes from the original 2002 Xenon drawing. All 15 weights remain unknown: no complete-brakeset weight, bare-arm figure or unpublished total is substituted. See the [caliper guide](brake-calipers.md).

SRAM RB-RED-A1, RB-RED-B1, RB-RED-B2, RB-FRC-A1, RB-RIV-A1 and RB-APX-A1 are complete rim-caliper models in the [manufacturer's 2020 spare-parts book](https://www.sram.com/globalassets/document-hierarchy/spare-parts/spare-parts-catalog-archive/2020-sram-spare-parts-catalog.pdf#page=34). They represent front/rear pairs with explicit unknown weights. SB-RED-A1 is a separate left/right DoubleTap control pair. Hydraulic control-system masses are not repurposed as caliper weights.

BB-DUB-BSA-A1 and BB-DUB-PF-A1 are shared SRAM bottom-bracket families with [manufacturer specifications](https://www.sram.com/en/sram/models/bb-dub-bsa-a1) and documented [Road AXS/XPLR installation options](https://docs.sram.com/s/RoadAXSandXPLRAXSUserManual.html). The frame determines shell type and spacers; the inventory lists alternatives in the documented system. Their weights remain null because the product-page reference does not establish the selected road shell/spacer assembly. In particular, the PressFit page's BB92 figure is not assigned to a road PF86.5 configuration.

## Derailleur reference configurations

The Shimano 2012, 2014–2015, 2017–2018 and 2025–2026 specification handbooks provide model/cage columns. The smallest cassette sprocket comes from **minimum top sprocket**, never minimum low sprocket. Conditional cells and footnotes were checked before choosing a reference:

- RD-4700-GS uses the front-double reference: 41T capacity, 34T largest, 11T smallest and 16T front difference. The source separately gives a 32T largest sprocket and 20T front difference for a triple.
- RD-M7000-11 GS uses a 2×11 reference, with a 42T largest sprocket. Its separate 46T allowance belongs to a single chainring.
- RD-M8050 GS and RD-M9050 SGS use 2×11 references, with the manufacturer's Di2 cross-chain restriction retained in the description. Neither record borrows the single-chainring 46T maximum.
- A dash or “single” in a table is not converted to an invented numeric zero. Explicit manufacturer `0T` values remain valid numeric references.

The original Campagnolo 2002 technical tables were checked visually and matched to complete codes in the 2002 parts book. They provide six complete limit sets and six weights: Record RD00-RE210 187 g, RD00-RE210L 193 g; Chorus RD00-CH210 207 g, RD00-CH210L 225 g; Centaur RD02-CE209 and RD02-CE210 223 g each. Medium-cage minimum sprockets are 13T in the Record/Chorus rows; they are not copied from the short-cage columns.

The Campagnolo ten-/eleven-speed manual separates the pre-2015 eleven-speed family from later Revolution 11+ components. Those earlier limits apply to the matching Athena references and the named Veloce/Centaur ten-speed cage variants. Twelve-speed mechanical RD20 records use the 29–34-marked cage reference in the [2022 manufacturer manual](https://www.campagnolo.com/on/demandware.static/-/Library-Sites-campagnoloLibrary/default/dwf6239c9f/pdf/035_2397_Technical%20manual%20-%2012s_Mechanical%20units%20rear%20derailleur%20-%20Campagnolo_Rev06_10_22_ENG.pdf#page=3). Wireless twelve-speed Super Record and Super Record S use their own technical-manual rows, separately from thirteen-speed and Nano Clutch variants.

The [SRAM 2010 technical manual](https://www.sram.com/globalassets/document-hierarchy/user-manuals/sram-mtb/drivetrain/2010-sram-technical-manual.pdf#page=6) explicitly lists the original RED, Force and Rival ten-speed references at 33/28/11/16T. The Force and Rival records retain that original short-cage reference; it does not cover later WiFLi or eleven-speed assemblies.
