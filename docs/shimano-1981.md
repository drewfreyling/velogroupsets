# Shimano Bicycle System Components 1981: catalogue gap review

**22 November follow-up:** the [early-model import](shimano-early-followup-2026-11-22.md) resolves further inventory/document-link gaps under the optional-link policy, adding 26 components and five weights across the reviewed editions. The original review below records the earlier batch and its findings.


Reviewed on 21 November 2026 from the [owner-supplied original Shimano catalogue](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1nhlBEB76WGQO9qj5PtkOyTIPzvBFie6V/view). The cover explicitly says **1981**. The scan contains 59 PDF pages, including technical explanations, compatibility charts and product spreads. Page numbers below are **PDF pages**; the scan includes unnumbered material and does not have a constant offset from printed pagination.

The review adds **41 component records**, **two groupsets** and **18 newly sourced weights**: 17 in new records and one in the existing FD-AT12 record. The catalogue contains 259 groupsets and 1,518 components after this batch. Existing component IDs, slugs, model numbers and primary documents are preserved. BB-6210 gains an explicitly documented Deore touring membership; BB-7500 gains the listed 7500 track membership. Dura-Ace 10 gains its sourced **track** category correction.

Each new component has a Shimano-hosted assembly drawing or service instruction. The Drive scan is supporting manufacturer-publication evidence; it does not become an approved primary-document host. No PDF bytes or scan images are committed.

### Hub exclusions

The four HB-7020/HB-7520 references are solid-axle track assemblies with hub nuts, confirmed by their manufacturer drawings. They have no quick-release skewers. No nut/axle mass is subtracted, and no weighed hole count or rear spacing is invented.

The [Shimano FH-7261/FH-7271 service instruction SI-M-14](https://si.shimano.com/en/pdfs/si/M-14/SI-M-14-000-00-ENG.pdf) explicitly says its weights are measured **without sprockets or quick-release cam sections** and identifies 32-hole references. These are direct manufacturer weights, not deductions from the catalogue’s QR-unspecified figures. FH-7271 is established by this service instruction rather than a claim that it appears on the 1981 spread. The instruction’s front-hub figure is not assigned an invented model number.

The new FH-6251, FH-6261, FH-7250 and FH-7260 records retain unknown weights: “except cassette gears” does not establish skewer exclusion. Existing original Dura-Ace and 600 hub records are not assigned their QR-unspecified figures either.

## Groups and identities

- **Dura-Ace 10 7000** is explicitly headed “Track Ensemble” on PDF page 26. Its category is corrected to track; 10 refers to 10 mm chain pitch, not ten-speed. The existing ID and 1976 start reference are retained.
- **Dura-Ace Track 7500** is the earlier conventional half-inch-pitch offering on page 29. It is a separate group from the later 7600/7710 offering. Its explicitly listed BB-7500 reuses the existing shared bottom-bracket record. Its 1981 start records availability in this reviewed edition, not a newly proven first introduction date.
- **Deore Touring DE10/DE20** is the original touring range, with DD-compatible cranksets/pedals. Its category is road, not the later Deore MTB family. Its 1981 start likewise records reviewed availability, not an exact launch claim.
- Deore double cranks explicitly use the existing **BB-6210, 119 mm** assembly; triples use **BB-DE30, 121.5 mm**. BB-6210 is shared by membership, not duplicated.
- HB-7020 and HB-7520 use distinct front/rear IDs and slugs while retaining the actual unsuffixed manufacturer model number.
- Historical Altus LT/ST controls and derailleurs stay within the existing Altus Selecta road group. Compatibility with those controls is not used to attach them to later MTB Altus families.

## Derailleur limits

Deore RD-DE10 lists 30T total capacity, 30T largest sprocket and 20T front difference; RD-DE20 lists 34T total, 34T largest and 20T front difference. Neither page supplies a smallest-sprocket limit, which remains null.

FD-DE10 has 20T front capacity. FD-AT11 and FD-AT12 each list 14T front capacity. Short-cage RD-AT11 lists 28T general largest-sprocket compatibility, with a separate Shimano-only 30T allowance, as already documented for RD-AT12. This is not an unconditional 30T limit.

The long-cage RD-AT21/AT22 rows print a 34T largest sprocket alongside a 30T Shimano-only footnote. Their canonical largest-sprocket limits remain null pending clarification of that scope; the source note is retained. Front difference is explicitly 13T, while total capacity and smallest sprocket remain unknown. RD-6210 is named as the long-cage 600 EX variant without separate limits or mass; RD-6200’s numbers are not transferred to it.

## Figures deliberately withheld

- **Cranksets:** the Dura-Ace 10 comparison lists a right crank and chainring separately, not a complete left/right crankset with arm length. Other reviewed crank spreads do not give compliant crankset-only reference weights. No bottom-bracket or missing-left-crank deductions are used.
- **Shifters:** SL-7220, SL-DE20/21 and SL-AT11/21/22/23 are catalogued as complete pairs with null weights. The mass figures do not explicitly identify complete pair references; the availability of single controls is listed separately. No side is doubled.
- **Pedals:** PD-DE10 has a 570 g figure without explicit pair basis. Its pair record remains unweighed. PD-7200’s 173 g reference includes a toe clip and strap but does not establish a complete pair and is not doubled.
- **Front derailleurs:** the shared 102 g FD-7200/7210 figure does not identify which mount was weighed. Their existing unknown weights are retained.
- **Cantilever brakes:** BR-6102’s 338 g figure does not establish a complete front/rear set. Its new pair record remains unweighed; pads/bolts are not asserted for an unknown canonical weight.
- **Bottom brackets:** BB-7500 and the newly identified BB-DE30/BB-SL31 assemblies have no qualified manufacturer weight in the reviewed material. BB-SL31 is identified as Selecta Octa Joint, not relabelled square taper.

## Remaining source and taxonomy gaps

The scan identifies these useful leads, but they are not given unrelated or generic primary technical documents merely to add records:

- Original Dura-Ace **RD-7100 (174 g), FD-7100 (110 g clamp), FC-7110, BB-7200 and HP-7100**, PDF pages 38–41. Public manufacturer-document searches did not find exact current documents. The old 1973 “Original” group is not used to claim these numbered parts existed at its launch.
- Original 600 **RD-6100 (235 g), SL-BC10 (160 g explicitly per pair) and SL-QB11**, pages 42–43; exact manufacturer-hosted documents were not found. The SL-BC10 pair figure is a particularly useful lead, not a rejected single-control weight.
- **PD-7200**, page 31, and **SL-AT12**, page 55, likewise need exact manufacturer-hosted assembly documents.
- Large-flange **FH-6253/6263**, page 35, and the Selecta **FH-Q510/Q610, FH-N510/N610 and FH-K610** families, page 57, need exact manufacturer documents and configuration review. The latter also introduce system-specific compatibility details that are not guessed from adjacent charts.
- Chains, standalone threaded freewheels/fixed track sprockets, cable guides, fork ends and stems are outside the present component taxonomy. Their printed weights do not become cassette or headset records. The Positron/FF charts need a separate system/model review before adding groupsets from them.

These gaps are explicit research leads, not a claim that the scan lacks the parts or that every source problem has been resolved.

## Later source clarification

The [April 1982 follow-up](shimano-1982.md#altus-limits-resolved) resolves the RD-AT21/AT22 footnote ambiguity with separate long-cage specification blocks: both list 34T total capacity and 34T largest sprocket. It also supplies the short-cage RD-AT11/AT12 28T total capacities. The earlier findings above describe the original 1981 review, not the current unresolved status.
