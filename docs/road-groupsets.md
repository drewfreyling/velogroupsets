# Additional road groupsets

The 9 November 2026 review adds **75 groupset records**: 29 Shimano and 46 Campagnolo. SRAM already has its main road families represented in the [SRAM guide](sram.md); four Wide front-derailleur/crankset options extend its existing Force and Rival AXS records. The catalogue now contains **158 groupsets and 808 component identities across three manufacturers**.

This is a review of named road families and drivetrain generations, including historical road equipment and newer recreational drop-bar systems. It does not treat every finish, crank length, limited-edition presentation or power-meter option as a separate groupset. Existing SRAM XPLR records remain; this review does not add MTB, track, gravel-only GRX or Ekar families. Later disc-brake controls and revised parts belong to their documented family unless the drivetrain system itself is distinct.

**Component coverage is incomplete.** The review added 209 component identities with initially unknown weights, and 36 of the newly added groupsets currently have no verified component list. A group entry establishes a documented range and date; it does not imply that a complete build kit has been catalogued. Manufacturer-hosted technical documents, exact assembly codes and membership still need review before further component records are added. The existing archive exceptions are unchanged.

## Dates and identifiers

A start year is the documented introduction where a launch or manufacturer history establishes it. Where only a dated reference edition has been reviewed, it is a conservative availability year, not a claim that no earlier equipment existed. A start year never dates all later component options to the original launch.

- [Shimano’s 600 history](https://www.shimano.com/en/100th/history/products/17.html) identifies the original 1975 group, Arabesque EX in 1978, AX in 1980 and New EX in 1983. Shimano’s [company history](https://www.shimano.com/en/company/history.html) dates Santé to 1986. The early [Altus history](https://www.shimano.com/en/100th/history/products/result.html?id=21) describes drop-bar sports-bike equipment; these Selecta/AX entries do not contain modern Altus MTB parts.
- Shimano’s [Tiagra](https://www.shimano.com/en/100th/history/products/result.html?id=73), [Sora](https://www.shimano.com/en/100th/history/products/result.html?id=74) and [Claris](https://www.shimano.com/en/100th/history/products/result.html?id=97) timelines establish the generation dates. Tiagra’s family introduction is 1999 although its gallery labels 4400 as 2000; this record uses the family introduction. Sora 3300 follows the manufacturer’s 2000 timeline, and 3400 its 2007 timeline, rather than treating an earlier service drawing as a retail-launch date. R4000 uses the manufacturer’s March 2026 announcement.
- Road 2200 and A050 use the reviewed 2005 line-up as their availability reference; these are not verified first-launch years. The four Ultegra Di2 families distinguish ten-speed 6770, eleven-speed 6870/R8050 and twelve-speed R8100. R8100 names the group series; RD-R8150, FD-R8150 and ST-R8170 retain their actual component numbers.
- Campagnolo’s original Gran Sport 1003 record uses the circa-1955 catalogue reference, Record Strada circa 1958, and Nuovo Record circa 1967. The classic Super Record and Nuovo Gran Sport entries use the reviewed 1974 catalogue; 1974 is an availability reference, not a newly asserted first introduction. These early catalogue dates can be refined with additional evidence.
- Daytona nine-speed and ten-speed, Centaur nine-speed and ten-speed, Veloce nine-speed and ten-speed, and electronic/mechanical Record systems are separate records. Veloce was still nine-speed in the reviewed 2003 parts edition; its ten-speed record uses the 2004 range. The Champ/Race/Comp Triple entries use the 2007 equipment documented in the September 2007 trade catalogue for the 2008 range; their earliest launch dates remain unverified. Racing Triple nine-speed includes the family later named Racing T rather than duplicating that rename.
- Campagnolo’s 2009 eleven-speed range was announced in 2008. EPS debuted in 2011, Athena EPS in 2012 and Chorus EPS in 2014 for the 2015 range. Record/Super Record mechanical twelve-speed, wired EPS twelve-speed, wireless twelve-speed and wireless thirteen-speed remain distinct. Ultra is the named upgrade offering; the entry does not invent a different rear derailleur. Group `model_number` values such as “11-speed”, “Original” or “Strada” identify a documented system when there is no reviewed numeric complete-group SKU; they are not invented manufacturer part numbers.
- Drop-bar CUES U6030/U3030 and ESSA U2030 date to the 2025 drop-bar introduction, not their earlier flat-bar relatives. Later reference options are not all described as launch equipment. Match the actual speed variant: U3030 has different complete nine- and ten-speed lever pairs, and U6030 includes ten/eleven-speed options.

## Component sources and identity

Shimano’s manufacturer-hosted line-up charts supply membership, with the exact PDF page retained in each record. Exploded drawings from Shimano’s service API supply model-specific technical documents. Cassettes, bottom brackets and hubs already present in the catalogue retain their IDs and original weight references when a new membership is established. The shared Ultegra cranks, cassettes and hubs are not duplicated for Di2.

Campagnolo’s original **2017 spare-parts catalogue, part D** supplies complete assembly codes and explicit group assignments. The [archived original PDF](https://www.bikeschematics.com/wp-content/uploads/schematics/Campagnolo/Campagnolo%20Spare%20Parts%202017%20-%20Part%20D.pdf) was reviewed alongside the original 2013/2015/2018 part-A drawings. Manufacturer technical-document links point to the corresponding part-A/B editions listed in [Campagnolo’s documentation catalogue](https://www.campagnolo.com/gb-en/technical-support-area/documentation/). The media host returned connection errors from this environment, so its delivery was not independently confirmed; the manufacturer’s listing and the matching archived edition establish the document identity. The archive is a supplemental fact source, not a new `technical_document` exception.

Only complete component codes are imported: replacement screws, cages, stiffness increasers, clamp adapters and chain guards are not mistaken for complete derailleurs. The selection includes rear/front derailleurs, complete Ergopower pairs, 170 mm cranksets, selected cassettes, threaded bottom-bracket cups and explicitly assigned Record hubs. Shared cups, cassettes and hubs retain one identity across only the assignments printed in the table. Compatibility alone is insufficient. The 2017 reference parts must not all be described as original 2008/2011 launch equipment.

The manufacturer’s wireless compatibility table supplies EP23-SRD12, RD23-SR12WRL, FC23-SR12 and CS23-SR12 markings. These records describe the original Super Record Wireless reference only. Matching markings in the Super Record S compatibility column do not establish identical complete assemblies, so Super Record S membership is not inferred. FD23-SR12WRL is the earlier mounting reference; the later FD01 dual-position version is not silently merged into it. Modern Chorus/Centaur/Super Record component records also cite the retailer/distributor used to identify the actual MPN alongside their manufacturer specification/manual. Manufacturer website product IDs are not substituted for physical model numbers.

SRAM’s four Wide records use the actual FD-FRC-EW-D1, FD-RIV-EW-D1, FC-FRC-W-D1 and FC-RIV-W-D1 Model IDs. The 47.5 mm chainline and 43/30T reference are documented manufacturer options within existing Force/Rival AXS families. No new groupset is created merely for the Wide option, and its later introduction does not change the parent family’s start year.

## Weights, pairs and limits

The initial review left all 209 new weights null. Subsequent [contributor weight reports](contributor-weights.md) and the [catalogue/handbook review](dealer-catalogues.md) supply individually cited measurements, manufacturer weights and derailleur limits. No retail figure, single control’s weight, complete hydraulic brake-system weight or undocumented skewer deduction is promoted to a compliant component weight. Shifters are complete left/right pairs; the ESSA pair includes a brake-only left lever. Front-derailleur clamp diameters, crank configurations and cassette gearing are recorded only where supported by the exact source. Future measurements must follow the [weight guidelines](weights-guidelines.md).

Every added rear derailleur has the four required limit fields. Unknown limits remain null, with no cage-based or arithmetic inference. The Super Record thirteen-speed road reference uses the manufacturer’s explicit 41T capacity, 36T largest sprocket, 10T smallest sprocket and 16T front difference. A generic technical manual’s other cage rows are not applied to an unspecified older assembly. Component coverage and unknown values remain visible in catalogue pages and the search index.

## Remaining review

The source table identifies groups with no verified component list. Other variants remain missing even where a group has components: historical Campagnolo and early Shimano assemblies, additional triple controls, complete contemporary twelve/thirteen-speed Campagnolo configurations, cage revisions, brakes outside the supported taxonomy and compliant weights. Rare regional ranges and limited editions have not been exhaustively surveyed. This review expands documented road coverage; it is not a claim that every road groupset and component ever produced is present.
